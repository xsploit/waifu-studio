# SOMA / Hikari synchronized motion comparison

Local diagnostic, 2026-10-30. This evaluates one experimental MotionBricks walking sequence, not the full animation library. No product animation, default, or collision setting was changed.

## What was compared

- The exact 76 saved frames (30 Hz, two chained segments) were reconstructed on NVIDIA's original 77-joint SOMA skeleton and skinned source mesh, and captured on Hikari's wardrobe VRM through Waifu Studio's production VRMA retargeter. Frame 26 repeats the segment boundary.
- Hikari was captured continuously in the offscreen production renderer with spring simulation active. We captured all 76 frames from front, side, rear, and the opposite side, both with the experimental arm guard off and on.
- The source mesh reconstruction was independently checked against NVIDIA's own FK and skinning kernels at frames 0, 9, 25, 26 and 75: maximum vertex difference about 0.000004 m. The source visual is a local review asset, not a redistribution asset. SOMA is untextured and unclothed, whereas Hikari wears a skirt and jacket.
- [Open the local frame scrubber](../work/motionbricks_probe/comparison/index.html): the slider, arrow keys, and play button compare the same frame. Toggle the guard and Hikari's joint/collider overlay. The colored skeleton and gold collider circles are X-ray projections, so they do not prove depth or surface contact. [Guard on/off sheet](../work/motionbricks_probe/comparison/guard_comparison.jpg). Close-up sheets cover every frame under `work/motionbricks_probe/comparison/sheets`.
- The page's selection logic and all 304 referenced frame images were checked with `node work/motionbricks_probe/comparison/verify_review.cjs`. The page was not tested in an interactive browser session.

## Findings

1. **The guard-off arm motion is close to the source on this walk.** Across 76 frames, the difference in torso-relative upper- and lower-arm direction averages approximately 4.6–5.5 degrees depending on the segment, with a maximum around 6.3 degrees. The source and destination elbow bend differ by much less in the sampled summary. This comparison uses bone landmarks; the characters have different proportions, meshes and bind poses. It does not validate other clips or exclude wrist/finger, clothing, or gaze defects.
2. **The hand/skirt problem is real in 3D.** A separate diagnostic skinned Hikari's selected triangles using the actual captured skin matrices and morph weights, and tested transverse triangle crossings. At frames 9, 25, 45 and 58, it found crossings between arm/hand-weighted skin and the bottoms-material surfaces. The images show hands disappearing into or protruding through the skirt at several points. Some parts of the skirt move via the existing springs; that is not enough to keep the animated hand outside it.
3. **The guard solves the sampled crossings by visibly damaging the pose.** With the guard on, that same hand-skin/bottoms subset had no detected transverse crossings at those four frames. This is not a proof of complete clearance. Relative to guard off, the guard displaced a hand by up to 33 cm on the left and 27 cm on the right over the sequence, changed upper-arm directions by up to approximately 42–44 degrees, and changed measured elbow bend by up to approximately 34–48 degrees. The guard-on captures visibly raise the arms away from the source gait. It fails the visual acceptance criterion and should remain disabled.
4. **The preview exporter loses a small amount of source head motion.** It writes rotations for mapped joints only. The source `Neck2` has approximately 2.6–4.5 degrees of local motion relative to its rest pose in this sequence, but it receives no animated track in the preview export. The source head landmark reconstructed from the imported preview differs from the unexported raw source by up to about 1 cm. This should be corrected in a real adapter, but it does not explain the sampled hand/skirt crossings. Eye and jaw channels were excluded.

The crossing diagnostic detects only selected transverse surface intersections. It can miss contained geometry, coplanar overlaps, and intersections between frames; it may count intersecting seams or the same crossing more than once. Its raw counts are not a quality score. The result is supported by the actual rendered frames and the specific hand-skin versus bottoms-material pair, not by total crossing counts alone. Guard-on and guard-off runs independently simulate secondary motion.

## What this changes

Do not replace VRM or enable the current guard as a fix. For this sequence, focus the next prototype on Hikari-specific hand/forearm and skirt interaction while keeping the source arm movement recognizable. Test candidate corrections against the same saved frames and actual skinned surfaces, including transitions and motion between samples. Reject candidates that pass intersection tests by lifting or bending the arms unnaturally. Investigate whether the skirt springs and authored hand colliders are positioned and sized effectively in the running app, and compare clothing behavior separately from arm correction. A faithful source adapter should also handle the unmapped neck chain before any general MotionBricks integration.

This does not establish that all 157 animations are fixed, that every animation retargets correctly, that real-time MotionBricks is integrated, or that native Linux GPU output has been verified.
